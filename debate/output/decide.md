**Decision: The Opposition is more convincing.**

The opposition presents the stronger case overall because it responds more directly and effectively to the central policy question: whether the risks of autonomous AI trading justify a full ban. Its arguments are clearer, more balanced, and more persuasive in showing that the harms identified by the proposition can be managed through regulation rather than prohibition.

### Why the Opposition was stronger
- **Better proportionality:** The opposition repeatedly makes the crucial point that risk does not automatically justify banning an entire technology. That is a strong logical framing, especially in finance, where many risky tools are already permitted under oversight.
- **More practical solution:** It offers concrete alternatives—circuit breakers, audit trails, liability rules, risk limits, kill switches, and surveillance—which makes its position feel workable rather than merely idealistic.
- **Stronger rebuttal to accountability concerns:** The opposition convincingly argues that autonomy does not eliminate responsibility, because institutions and executives can still be held legally accountable. This directly weakens one of the proposition’s main points.
- **Persuasive fairness argument:** It also makes a credible case that a ban could entrench existing large firms and reduce competition, undermining the proposition’s claim that prohibition would protect fairness.
- **Clearer policy logic:** The opposition avoids absolutism and instead emphasizes governance, which is a more nuanced and realistic approach.

### Where the Proposition was weaker
The proposition raises serious concerns—market instability, accountability gaps, manipulation, and fairness—but it relies heavily on worst-case scenarios and treats them as sufficient grounds for a blanket ban. That makes the argument feel somewhat overstated. It identifies real risks, but it does not convincingly prove that regulation would be inadequate or that all autonomous trading agents should be prohibited rather than tightly controlled.

### Final judgment
The proposition makes a serious and coherent warning case, but the opposition is ultimately more convincing because it offers a stronger, more balanced, and more policy-sound response: autonomous AI in financial trading should be regulated, not banned.
